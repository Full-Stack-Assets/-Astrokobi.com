In a playful twist on science outreach, the European Space Agency has announced a limited‑time partnership with The Pokémon Company International for World Space Week, running 4–10 October 2026. The collaboration will blend ESA’s space missions with Pokémon’s imaginative universe, inviting fans to explore the cosmos through familiar characters. By merging storytelling and real‑world space achievements, the initiative aims to spark curiosity among young audiences across Europe and beyond.

<Callout type="takeaway">ESA and Pokémon are joining forces to use pop‑culture storytelling to inspire the next generation about space.</Callout>

## What happened

ESA and The Pokémon Company International revealed that they will co‑create a series of limited‑edition digital and physical assets, educational activities, and social‑media content throughout World Space Week. The partnership, announced on 4 August 2026, emphasizes shared values of curiosity and imagination, aligning with ESA’s Strategy 2040 to inspire Europe’s youth.

The collaboration will feature a special Pokémon‑themed space helmet, a Pokédex‑style guide to ESA’s missions, and interactive online events where participants can learn about satellite technology and planetary exploration. Details on the exact deliverables are forthcoming, but the agencies promise that fans can “fasten their space helmets and get their Pokédexes ready.”

## Why it matters

Linking a globally recognized entertainment brand with a leading space agency creates a powerful conduit for science communication, reaching children and families who might not otherwise engage with space topics. By framing ESA’s work within the familiar narrative of Pokémon, the partnership can boost STEM interest, support ESA’s outreach goals, and demonstrate how imagination fuels real scientific discovery. It also showcases Europe’s commitment to innovative public engagement ahead of upcoming missions.

## How to think about it

Educators can use the Pokémon‑themed materials as an entry point for lessons on orbital mechanics, satellite design, or planetary science, linking game concepts to real data. Parents and hobbyists can explore the online events together with children, turning play into a shared learning experience. For outreach professionals, the partnership serves as a case study on blending pop culture with factual science to broaden impact.
