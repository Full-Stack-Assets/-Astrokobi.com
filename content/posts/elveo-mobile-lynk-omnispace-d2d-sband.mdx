On August 17, Lynk Global completed its merger with Omnispace and rebranded the combined entity as Elveo Mobile. The new company now controls 60 MHz of S‑band spectrum and a fleet of seven low‑Earth‑orbit satellites that already provide intermittent direct‑to‑device messaging. Regulatory approvals in the United States and abroad cleared the way for the transaction, which was first announced in October. By pairing Omnispace’s spectrum with Lynk’s D2D platform, Elveo aims to move beyond simple alerts toward bandwidth‑heavy applications. This shift could reshape how mobile network operators deliver connectivity from space.

<Callout type="takeaway">Elveo Mobile’s merger gives it the spectrum and satellite assets needed to scale direct‑to‑device services beyond basic messaging.</Callout>

## What happened
Elveo Mobile announced that it has secured all required U.S. and foreign regulatory approvals, officially closing the Lynk‑Omnispace merger first reported in October. The combined entity inherits Omnispace’s 60 MHz of S‑band spectrum and Lynk’s existing seven‑satellite LEO constellation, which has been used for intermittent messaging and alerts in a handful of island nations.

Previously, Omnispace planned to deploy more than 600 satellites using the S‑band, but those plans stalled after interference reports involving SpaceX’s Starlink Mobile service, which operates on cellular frequencies in the United States. While Lynk’s network has relied on partnerships with mobile network operators (MNOs) to use cellular bands, the dedicated S‑band allocation is expected to support higher‑throughput data applications. Elveo’s CEO, Ramu Potarazu, indicated that strategic partnerships with over 50 MNOs in 60+ countries will be announced later this year, alongside manufacturing and launch updates for next‑generation D2D satellites.

## Why it matters
The merger creates a rare blend of spectrum rights and orbital assets, positioning Elveo Mobile as a direct competitor to SpaceX’s Starlink Mobile and AST SpaceMobile. Dedicated S‑band spectrum reduces dependence on congested cellular bands, potentially enabling more reliable and higher‑capacity services such as voice, video, and broadband from space. For mobile operators, the partnership offers a pathway to extend coverage to remote or underserved regions without building extensive ground infrastructure. Moreover, the involvement of SES as a major shareholder provides access to geostationary and medium‑Earth‑orbit platforms, hinting at a multi‑orbit strategy that could deliver continuous global coverage by 2027.

## How to think about it
Industry observers should track three key signals: (1) the timeline for Elveo’s next‑generation satellite production and launch schedule, (2) announced partnership agreements with mobile operators, and (3) regulatory developments concerning S‑band usage in different jurisdictions. Evaluating these factors will help gauge when Elveo can transition from basic alert messaging to robust data services and how it may influence the broader D2D market.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that Elveo Mobile will offer high‑throughput broadband immediately; the rollout of next‑gen satellites and spectrum coordination will take time.</Callout>
