Every week ESA curates a visual digest of its latest activities, and the 13‑17 July 2026 edition is packed with highlights. Astronauts from NASA’s Artemis II mission toured the ESTEC campus, shaking hands with the European Service Module team. Engineers performed a critical fit‑check of the Meteosat Third Generation‑I2 satellite with its launch adapter, while a new green chemical propulsion test fired for the first time. The collection also delivers fresh views of the Beta Pictoris system, the floor of Mars’s Kaiser Crater, and a Sentinel‑2 snapshot of Guinea‑Bissau.

<Callout type="takeaway">ESA’s weekly images illustrate how operational milestones and scientific observations intertwine across agencies.</Callout>

## What happened

Artemis II astronauts Christina Koch, Reid Wiseman and Victor Glover, joined by CSA astronaut Jeremy Hansen, visited ESTEC and met the European Service Module team that will power the Orion spacecraft on future lunar missions.

At the same time, ESA engineers completed a “fit check” of the MTG‑I2 satellite with its launch‑vehicle adapter, confirming mechanical clearances ahead of the scheduled launch later this year. The new green chemical propulsion laboratory also performed its inaugural test firing, demonstrating a low‑toxicity propellant concept.

The image set includes a near‑infrared IFU observation of the Beta Pictoris system from JWST’s NIRSpec, a high‑resolution view of the floor of Mars’s Kaiser Crater, and a Copernicus Sentinel‑2 composite showing the Republic of Guinea‑Bissau.

## Why it matters

The astronaut visit underscores the deepening partnership between NASA and ESA, vital for the Artemis program’s success. The MTG‑I2 fit check reduces launch risk, ensuring the satellite can deliver critical weather data for Europe and beyond. New scientific images enrich our understanding of exoplanet environments and Martian geology, while the Sentinel‑2 snapshot aids regional monitoring in West Africa.
