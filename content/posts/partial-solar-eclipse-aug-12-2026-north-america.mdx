On August 12, 2026 a partial solar eclipse will sweep across the northern United States and Canada. The Moon will cover between 3 % and 53 % of the Sun’s disk depending on location. This event follows a series of recent total eclipses that have drawn large crowds across the continent. Skywatchers who are far enough north can experience a brief “sliver” of darkness in the middle of the day. Observing the eclipse safely requires certified eclipse glasses.

<Callout type="takeaway">A partial solar eclipse will sweep across northern U.S. and Canada on Aug 12, 2026, offering a brief glimpse of the Sun’s disk for observers with proper glasses.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s eclipse calculations show that the Moon’s umbral shadow will miss North America, but the penumbral shadow will pass over the northern tier of the continent. Totality will begin near the North Pole and travel southward across Greenland, Iceland, Spain and Portugal, while North America receives only a partial phase.

In the United States and Canada, coverage ranges from a low of 3 % in Detroit to a high of 53 % in St. John’s, Newfoundland. The partial eclipse will begin in Anchorage at 7:36 a.m. local time, peak at 8:21 a.m., and end at 9:09 a.m. Similar timing windows occur across the continent, for example Bangor, Maine sees peak coverage at 1:53 p.m., and New York City peaks at 1:54 p.m. Other listed locations include Montreal (18 % at 1:45 p.m.), Toronto (8 % at 1:58 p.m.), Philadelphia (7 % at 1:53 p.m.), and Detroit (3 % at 1:36 p.m.).

## Why it matters
The eclipse offers a rare daylight spectacle for residents of northern latitudes who have not experienced a total eclipse in recent memory. Public interest can boost local tourism and encourage community outreach by astronomy clubs. At the same time, the event highlights the need for eye‑safety education, as partial eclipses are often mistaken for safe viewing without protection.

## How to think about it
Identify the nearest city on the NASA list and note the start, peak, and end times. Check local weather forecasts for clear skies and plan to be in an open area with an unobstructed view of the Sun. Acquire certified eclipse glasses well before the event and keep them handy. If you want to photograph the eclipse, use a solar filter on your camera and practice safe viewing techniques ahead of time.

<Callout type="warning">Never look directly at the Sun without certified eclipse glasses; doing so can cause permanent eye damage.</Callout>
