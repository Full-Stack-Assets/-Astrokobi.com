Houston residents have been living through a patchwork of brief showers and dry spells all summer. Meteorologists now expect the daily thunderstorm coverage to inch upward through the coming weekend, with a noticeable uptick on Sunday. The gradual increase not only brings relief from the oppressive heat but also sets the stage for heavier rain events and flash‑flood concerns early next week.

<Callout type="takeaway">Storm coverage will rise this weekend, and flash‑flood risk grows by early next week.</Callout>

## What happened
Isolated to scattered afternoon thunderstorms are forecast for today and tomorrow, with many locations staying dry while a few pockets could see an inch or two of rain. Highs will hover in the low‑90s, and Sunday’s storm coverage is expected to be a touch higher than Saturday’s.

A high‑pressure ridge building over the Northern Plains and Midwest will position southeast Texas on the southern edge of the ridge, opening the “door” to abundant Gulf moisture. Combined with an upper‑level disturbance over the Deep South and a stalled weak front, this setup will enhance convection on Monday and Tuesday, creating pockets of heavy rain and a level‑2/4 slight‑risk for flash flooding in the eastern part of the area.

Rain totals this week have already varied widely, with some neighborhoods—from the East End through Galena Park and the Cloverleaf—recording 2 to 4 inches of rain, while other parts have seen little to none.

## Why it matters
The rising storm coverage offers a reprieve from the stifling heat, but the associated moisture surge raises the likelihood of flash flooding, especially in low‑lying eastern neighborhoods. Flooding can disrupt traffic, damage homes, and strain drainage systems already taxed by earlier rains. Residents, commuters, and city services must stay alert as the threat evolves.

## How to think about it
Treat each thunderstorm as a short‑term event but keep an eye on the longer‑range trend toward Monday‑Tuesday. Check the National Weather Service alerts multiple times a day, especially if you live near flood‑prone streets. If you need to travel, allow extra time and avoid low‑lying roadways after heavy rain. Keep a basic emergency kit—flashlight, bottled water, and a charged phone—ready in case flash flooding develops quickly.

<Callout type="warning">Do not attempt to drive through standing water; even a few inches can sweep a vehicle away.</Callout>
