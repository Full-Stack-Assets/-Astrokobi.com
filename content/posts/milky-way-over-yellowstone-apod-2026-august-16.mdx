The night sky over Wyoming was captured in a stunning 16‑image panorama that blends the glittering band of the Milky Way with the vivid colors of Yellowstone’s Silex Spring. Taken in 2014, the APOD image shows the spring’s 10‑meter‑wide pool, its bacterial pigments, and rising steam against a backdrop of billions of stars. This juxtaposition of terrestrial geothermal activity and the galactic plane invites both awe and reflection on Earth’s own volcanic forces. As scientists monitor the Yellowstone hotspot, the image serves as a visual reminder of the planet’s dynamic interior. For astronomy fans, it’s a rare chance to see our galaxy framed by a living, breathing landscape.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Milky Way over Yellowstone highlights how Earth’s volcanic heart shares the sky with billions of distant stars.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day features a 2014 panorama composed of 16 overlapping exposures taken from the rim of Silex Spring in Yellowstone National Park. The spring, about 10 meters across, glows with orange, green and red hues produced by thermophilic bacteria that thrive in the hot water.

Steam billows from the spring, heated by the underlying magma of the Yellowstone hotspot, while the central band of the Milky Way arches overhead, illuminated by countless stars across the galactic disk.

The image also notes that the last major super‑volcanic eruption of the hotspot occurred roughly 640,000 years ago, an event that reshaped large portions of North America.

## Why it matters
Understanding the hotspot’s history is crucial because a future eruption could have continent‑wide climatic and ecological impacts, and the APOD image makes that risk tangible for the public. By linking a familiar natural wonder with the cosmic environment, the picture helps bridge planetary geology and astronomy, encouraging interdisciplinary curiosity. Moreover, the visual contrast underscores how human perception of the night sky is shaped by the landscapes beneath it.
