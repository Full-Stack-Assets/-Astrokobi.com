Amateur astronomer Joël Lapointe discovered a potential impact crater in Quebec's Côte-Nord region using Google Maps. Field investigations confirmed the crater, named Uhackatik, formed 390 million years ago. This find adds to Canada's lead in confirmed impact craters due to its preserved ancient bedrock.

    <Callout type="takeaway">The discovery of the Uhackatik crater highlights the importance of continued exploration and research into Earth's geological history.</Callout>

    ## What happened
    The Uhackatik crater was confirmed through geological evidence. China is also advancing in asteroid detection with a network of satellites and Earth-based telescopes. NASA's New Horizons mission revealed huge landslides on Pluto, providing clues about geological processes on icy bodies.

    ## Why it matters
    These discoveries and advances contribute to our understanding of the solar system and help protect Earth from potential asteroid impacts. The Planetary Society and others are working to advance science, technology, and advocacy for planetary defense.
