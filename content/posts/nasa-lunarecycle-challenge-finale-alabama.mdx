NASA is hosting the LunaRecycle Challenge finale on Aug. 28 at the University of Alabama’s Lee Styslinger College of Engineering. Up to 14 finalist teams will present prototype and digital‑twin recycling systems designed to handle non‑metabolic waste on future lunar bases. The event includes a technology showcase, public voting, and the announcement of cash prizes totaling $1.325 million. By spotlighting these solutions, NASA hopes to accelerate sustainable resource management for Artemis and beyond.

<Callout type="takeaway">The LunaRecycle Challenge finale will spotlight prototype recycling systems that could enable longer‑duration lunar missions.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA will announce the winners of the final phase of the LunaRecycle Challenge on Friday, Aug. 28, beginning at 8:30 a.m. CDT in Room 1026 of H.M. Comer Hall on the UA campus. Media and the public are invited to a Technology Showcase where up to 14 teams will demonstrate their solutions before a live awards ceremony at noon.

Launched in 2024, the $3 million, two‑phase competition tasks teams with designing recycling concepts that reduce non‑metabolic waste for long‑duration lunar missions. In Phase 2’s final round, teams refined prototype and digital‑twin designs for live testing at McAbee Construction earlier this month. After the showcase, up to 11 teams will share $1.325 million in prize money, with the top two prototypes receiving $475 000 and $200 000, and the top two digital twins receiving $275 000 and $125 000. Additional Technical Achievement Prizes and a $25 000 People’s Choice Award will also be awarded.

## Why it matters
Effective waste‑recycling is a linchpin for sustainable lunar habitats; without it, resupply missions become prohibitively expensive. By incentivizing novel recycling architectures, the LunaRecycle Challenge accelerates technology readiness for Artemis and future commercial lunar outposts. The public voting element also builds broader stakeholder engagement, fostering a culture of shared responsibility for space sustainability.

## How to think about it
Stakeholders should view the showcase as a scouting opportunity: assess each prototype’s scalability, material compatibility with lunar regolith, and integration potential with existing life‑support systems. Follow‑on testing plans and partnerships with commercial lunar service providers will be key indicators of a solution’s path to flight.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the announced prototypes are flight‑ready; they still require extensive lunar‑environment testing before deployment.</Callout>
