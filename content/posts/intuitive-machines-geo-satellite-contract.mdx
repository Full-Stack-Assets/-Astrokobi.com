Intuitive Machines, best known for its lunar landers, announced on Aug 13 that it secured a contract worth over $600 million for three geostationary communications satellites. The award, signed with an undisclosed customer in June, will be built on the company’s IM 1300 bus, a platform inherited from its acquisition of Lanteris Space Systems. The satellites are scheduled for delivery over the next 30 months, aligning with the industry‑wide push to replace C‑band capacity cleared by the FCC. The deal highlights that commercial GEO demand remains robust even as Intuitive Machines expands beyond lunar services.

<Callout type="takeaway">Intuitive Machines’ $600 million GEO contract underscores the growing market for its IM 1300 satellite platform.</Callout>

## What happened
Intuitive Machines disclosed in its second‑quarter earnings release that it signed a $600 million contract for three geostationary communications satellites with an undisclosed customer in June. The satellites will use the IM 1300 series bus, the company’s flight‑proven platform that originated from the former Lanteris Space Systems acquisition. Delivery is planned over a 30‑month period, though the company provided no further details about the buyer or the intended orbital slots.

The announcement comes as the FCC finalizes the clearing of 160 MHz of upper C‑band spectrum, prompting operators such as SES, Eutelsat and Telesat to procure new satellites. While Intuitive Machines did not confirm a direct link, the timing suggests the contract could help satisfy the emerging demand for replacement capacity.

## Why it matters
The contract signals that Intuitive Machines’ GEO business is maturing, diversifying revenue beyond lunar missions and positioning the firm to compete for the wave of C‑band replacement satellites. A $600 million order provides a substantial cash flow boost and validates the IM 1300 platform against established manufacturers. However, the anonymity of the customer adds uncertainty, and the company must meet tight delivery schedules to align with regulatory deadlines.
