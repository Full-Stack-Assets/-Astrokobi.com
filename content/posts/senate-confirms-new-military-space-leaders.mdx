On August 7, 2026, the U.S. Senate confirmed Erich Hernandez‑Baquero as the assistant secretary of the Air Force for space acquisition and integration, and Roger Mason as the director of the National Reconnaissance Office. The appointments place two seasoned industry veterans at the helm of the nation’s military space procurement and intelligence‑satellite operations. The confirmations arrive as the Space Force prepares a record $71.1 billion fiscal‑2027 budget and seeks to overhaul its acquisition process. With the Senate also confirming Gen. Douglas Schiess as chief of space operations, the leadership overhaul signals a coordinated push to streamline capability delivery. Stakeholders from defense contractors to allied partners are watching how these changes will affect the speed and cost of future space systems.

<Callout type="takeaway">New Senate‑confirmed leaders are set to reshape U.S. military space acquisition and intelligence‑satellite operations.</Callout>

## What happened
The Senate voted 51‑47 to confirm Hernandez‑Baquero and Mason, adding them to a slate that also elevated Gen. Douglas Schiess to four‑star chief of space operations. Hernandez‑Baquero becomes the assistant secretary of the Air Force for space acquisition and integration, the senior civilian official overseeing the Space Force’s acquisition enterprise. His appointment marks the first Senate‑confirmed acquisition executive since Frank Calvelli left in early 2025.

Hernandez‑Baquero arrives from Raytheon, where he served as vice president for space intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance, and previously held senior roles at the NRO. In his confirmation hearing he emphasized acquisition reform, mission‑focused integration, and rebuilding the acquisition workforce. The Space Force is reorganizing its programs under Portfolio Acquisition Executives (PAEs) to push decision‑making closer to operational leaders.

Roger Mason, a retired Air Force colonel with extensive NRO experience, assumes leadership of the agency that operates the United States’ most sensitive intelligence satellites. His tenure is expected to continue the NRO’s push for rapid development cycles and tighter alignment with national security priorities.

## Why it matters
The new leadership arrives at a pivotal moment: the Space Force’s FY‑2027 budget request seeks a historic $71.1 billion, and Congress is pressuring faster delivery of resilient space capabilities amid great‑power competition. By consolidating acquisition authority under PAEs, Hernandez‑Baquero could shorten development timelines and reduce cost overruns that have plagued past programs. Mason’s stewardship of the NRO will influence how quickly next‑generation reconnaissance constellations become operational, directly affecting intelligence support for both combat and diplomatic missions.

## How to think about it
For analysts and industry partners, treat the leadership change as a signal to prioritize integrated, end‑to‑end solutions rather than isolated satellite contracts. Track upcoming PAE appointments, as they will become the primary decision‑makers for budget allocations. Companies should align proposals with the “capability‑first” language emphasized by Hernandez‑Baquero, highlighting how a system delivers operational outcomes across multiple mission sets. Meanwhile, watch for NRO procurement notices under Mason that may favor rapid‑prototype approaches and tighter data‑security requirements.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the new structure will instantly eliminate all acquisition delays; legacy contracts and congressional earmarks will still shape timelines.</Callout>
