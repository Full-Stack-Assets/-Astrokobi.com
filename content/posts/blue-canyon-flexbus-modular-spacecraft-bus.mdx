On August 26, 2026, RTX’s Blue Canyon Technologies unveiled FleXbus, a new modular spacecraft bus designed for rapid small‑sat integration. The FleXbus bundles a flight‑proven avionics computer, command‑and‑data handling, power‑management, and the company’s FleXcore GNC suite into a single kit. By offering a plug‑and‑play architecture, the system promises to cut development time and lower entry barriers for commercial and government missions. The announcement arrives as the small‑sat market accelerates, with dozens of new constellations seeking affordable, reliable platforms.
<Callout type="takeaway">FleXbus lets customers turn a set of proven components into a ready‑to‑fly spacecraft bus in weeks, not months.</Callout>
## What happened
Blue Canyon announced that FleXbus includes an integrated avionics computer, command and data handling system, GNC algorithms, sensors, actuators, an electrical power system controller, batteries, and standardized payload interfaces. Optional solar array drive assemblies, solar panels, hinges, as well as select radio and propulsion solutions can be added to tailor the bus to specific missions.
The company highlighted that the kit is flight‑proven, drawing on more than 160 spacecraft orders it has supported, and that customers can assemble the bus in their own facilities, preserving sovereign capability while reducing reliance on external integration services.
## Why it matters
The ability to field a complete spacecraft bus in weeks shortens the time‑to‑orbit for small‑sat operators, a critical advantage in a market where launch slots fill quickly. By leveraging Blue Canyon’s heritage components, users gain reliability without the cost of developing each subsystem from scratch. The modular approach also lowers the barrier for emerging space nations and commercial startups to build indigenous satellite capabilities.

## How to think about it
Start by mapping mission requirements—mass, power, data rate, and orbit—to the baseline FleXbus specifications. If the core kit meets those needs, use it as the primary architecture and select optional modules only where performance gaps exist. Allocate schedule margin for integration testing, even though the bus is pre‑qualified, to verify that custom payloads and any added propulsion or communications hardware operate as expected.
<Callout type="warning">Do not skip system‑level testing just because the bus is pre‑qualified; integration verification remains essential.</Callout>
