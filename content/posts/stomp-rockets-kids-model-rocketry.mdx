Stomp Rockets turn a simple stomp into a launch, letting kids send foam rockets skyward without any motors or chemicals. The new Ultra, Dueling, Space Collection, and X‑Treme sets offer heights from 200 ft to 400 ft and are marketed for ages five and up. Because the rockets rely on compressed air, they are safe, inexpensive, and easy to set up in a backyard or classroom. Introducing this hands‑on experience early can spark lasting interest in aerospace and STEM fields.

<Callout type="takeaway">Stomp Rockets provide a low‑cost, motor‑free gateway to real‑world rocketry for children.</Callout>

## What happened
Stomp Rocket kits consist of a launch base, a flexible hose, and foam rockets. When a user steps on the base, air is forced through the hose into the rocket’s nozzle, propelling it upward. The product line includes the Ultra model for ages 5+, a Dueling set that launches two rockets simultaneously, a Space Collection featuring NASA‑styled rockets, and the X‑Treme version that can reach up to 400 ft.

The Ultra and Dueling sets are rated for heights up to 200 ft, while the X‑Treme rockets claim a maximum of 400 ft. The Space Collection adds educational value with recognizable spacecraft designs, and the Glow Rockets provide visual fun in low light. Recommended age ranges span from 5 years for basic sets to 9‑13 years for the higher‑performance models.

## Why it matters
By removing the need for combustible propellants, Stomp Rockets lower the barrier to entry for hands‑on aerospace learning. Kids can experiment with launch angles, force, and aerodynamics in a safe environment, reinforcing physics concepts taught in school. The affordability and portability also make the kits suitable for after‑school programs, science fairs, and family backyard play, potentially widening participation in STEM.

## How to think about it
Parents should assess the launch space—clear at least 15 feet in front and 10 feet on the sides—and supervise younger children. Start with the basic Ultra set to teach the principle of air pressure, then progress to the Dueling or X‑Treme kits for competitive or higher‑altitude experiments. Pair launches with simple measurements (e.g., using a tape measure or smartphone app) to turn play into data collection, reinforcing scientific method skills.

<Callout type="warning">Never aim rockets at people, pets, or fragile objects; always launch in an open area away from windows and power lines.</Callout>
