SpaceX's public debut was a spectacle, but the company’s shares have since tumbled more than 40% from their peak. After soaring to about $225 post‑IPO, the stock now trades just below $112. Analysts point to a sharp rise in artificial‑intelligence capital expenditures as a key worry. The upcoming second‑quarter earnings on Aug 4 could confirm whether this spending is a growth engine or a financial drag. Understanding the balance between ambitious AI projects and short‑term profitability is crucial for anyone watching the firm’s trajectory.

<Callout type="takeaway">SpaceX's soaring AI investments could drive its already volatile stock even lower after the Aug 4 earnings.</Callout>

## What happened
SpaceX’s share price peaked near $225 after the historic IPO, yet it has slipped to just under $112 as of early August, reflecting a loss of more than 40% from its high. The decline follows a broader market trend where newly public space companies face intense scrutiny over earnings and cash burn.

In the first quarter, SpaceX allocated $7.7 billion to AI‑related capital expenditures, projecting an annual run rate of $30.8 billion. This marks a dramatic increase from the $12.7 billion AI capex recorded in 2025, signaling a rapid escalation in spending that outpaces the company’s reported revenue growth.

## Why it matters
Investors are wary when capex spikes without commensurate revenue gains, fearing that cash‑intensive projects could erode margins and depress earnings per share. A similar pattern recently hurt other tech giants, prompting sharp post‑earnings sell‑offs. For SpaceX, a weaker earnings report could tighten financing for its ambitious launch schedule, satellite constellations, and future Mars initiatives, potentially delaying mission timelines.

## How to think about it
Treat SpaceX’s upcoming earnings as a data point rather than a definitive verdict. Track the ratio of AI spend to overall revenue and watch for any disclosed timelines on AI‑driven product rollouts. Compare the company’s cash‑burn rate against its launch backlog to gauge whether the AI push is financially sustainable.
