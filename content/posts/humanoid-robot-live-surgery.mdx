In a groundbreaking achievement, a humanoid robot named Surgie successfully performed live surgery under the control of an expert surgeon. The robot removed a pig's gallbladder using standard surgical tools in an ordinary operating room. This milestone marks the debut of humanoid robots in standard surgical settings and paves the way for potential applications in minimally invasive surgery.

<Callout type="takeaway">A humanoid robot, controlled by a surgeon, successfully performed live surgery, demonstrating the potential for robots to amplify access to critical surgeries.</Callout>

## What happened
The study involved two successful surgeries where the teleoperated robot completed the task with minimal intervention from human surgeons, who remained on standby for emergencies. The robot, controlled by an expert surgeon, navigated to the gallbladder and gently removed it. Feedback from surgeons operating Surgie was positive, with reports of less physical strain and frustration, along with better overall performance.

## Why it matters
The use of humanoid robots in surgery has the potential to amplify access to critical surgeries, especially in remote communities where staffing is challenging or in austere environments like search and rescue scenarios. While robots have assisted surgeons for years, humanoid robots like Surgie offer a new level of flexibility and potential for minimally invasive procedures.
