In a move that deepens military cooperation between Beijing and Tehran, China has agreed to ship 400 shoulder‑fired air‑defence missile launchers to Iran. The deal, valued at roughly $70 million, includes Chinese‑made QW‑12 and FN‑16 man‑portable systems. Delivery of these short‑range weapons could significantly boost Iran’s ability to counter aerial threats. The announcement arrives amid heightened tensions between the United States and both nations, and it raises questions about regional stability. Observers note that the transaction follows months of U.S. intelligence warnings about Chinese arms transfers to Tehran.

<Callout type="takeaway">China’s $70 million sale of 400 man‑portable air‑defence launchers to Iran expands Tehran’s short‑range air‑defence capability.</Callout>

## What happened
China is sending 400 rocket launchers to Iran under a contract worth about $70 million. The agreement, signed by Hong‑Kong‑based Zhongqing Baoshang International Investment, covers the QW‑12 and FN‑16 man‑portable air‑defence missiles, which a single operator can fire at low‑altitude aircraft.

U.S. officials have long warned that China was preparing such a sale, and President Trump threatened “big problems” for Beijing if the deal proceeded. China’s foreign ministry dismissed the reports as “completely groundless,” while trade data show China continued importing roughly 1.6 million barrels of Iranian crude per day, a level comparable to early‑year figures.

## Why it matters
The influx of hundreds of Man‑PADs will markedly increase Iran’s short‑range air‑defence inventory, potentially complicating U.S. and allied air operations in the region. The sale also underscores a deepening strategic alignment between Beijing and Tehran, which could strain U.S.–China relations already fraught by trade and technology disputes. Regional actors, from Israel to Saudi Arabia, may reassess their security postures in response to Iran’s enhanced capability.
