The Hubble Space Telescope has captured a breathtaking image of a crimson cloud and stars in the stellar nursery LH 95. This region is located in the Large Magellanic Cloud, a dwarf galaxy that orbits the Milky Way. The image showcases blue and white stars shining against a backdrop of glowing gas.

<Callout type="takeaway">The image provides a glimpse into the formation of stars in a stellar association, where low-mass infant stars coexist with massive blue giant stars.</Callout>

## What happened
The Hubble Space Telescope observed the stellar nursery LH 95 on July 3, 2026. This region is part of the Large Magellanic Cloud, a dwarf galaxy that orbits the Milky Way. The image reveals a mix of blue and white stars set against a crimson background of glowing gas. These stars are part of a stellar association, a grouping of stars that form together.

LH 95 is home to both low-mass infant stars and massive blue giant stars. This diversity of stars is characteristic of stellar associations found in the Large Magellanic Cloud. The image was processed by Gladys Kober (NASA/Catholic University of America) using data from NASA, ESA, and researchers N. Da Rio (The University of Virginia), G. De Marchi (European Space Agency – ESTEC), and D. Gouliermis (Universitat Heidelberg).

## Why it matters
This image matters because it provides insights into the process of star formation in stellar nurseries. By studying regions like LH 95, astronomers can gain a better understanding of how stars form and evolve over time. The Large Magellanic Cloud is a valuable target for such studies due to its proximity to the Milky Way and its rich population of stars.

The observation of diverse stellar populations in LH 95 helps astronomers understand the conditions necessary for star formation. This knowledge can be applied to the study of star formation in other galaxies, contributing to a broader understanding of the universe.
