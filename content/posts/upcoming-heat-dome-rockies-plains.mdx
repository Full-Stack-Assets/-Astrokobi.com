A massive heat dome is poised to roll across the Rocky Mountains and the Great Plains later this week, promising record‑breaking temperatures. Meteorologists say the high‑pressure ridge will trap warm air aloft, preventing cooler breezes from reaching the surface. The phenomenon is expected to push daily highs well above seasonal norms, putting stress on power grids and vulnerable populations. Understanding the dome’s development helps residents prepare for the imminent heat wave.

## What happened
The high‑pressure ridge that forms a heat dome develops when atmospheric circulation stalls, allowing sunlight to continuously heat the same air mass. As the ridge strengthens, it acts like a lid, inhibiting vertical mixing and keeping cooler air trapped near the ground.
Forecasters have identified a broad swath of this ridge extending from the Rockies eastward into the Plains, where it will linger for several days. Satellite imagery shows the dome’s characteristic warm‑core signature, and surface observations already indicate rising temperatures.
## Why it matters
When a heat dome settles over a region, temperatures can soar far beyond normal, straining electricity demand as air‑conditioners run nonstop. Vulnerable groups—elderly, outdoor workers, and those without reliable cooling—face heightened health risks, including heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Agriculture also suffers as crops experience heat stress and soil moisture evaporates rapidly.

## How to think about it
Treat the heat dome as a short‑term climate extreme that requires immediate action. Check local heat advisories, adjust outdoor schedules to cooler morning or evening hours, and ensure you have access to water and shade. If you rely on air‑conditioning, verify that your system is functioning and consider backup power options. Communities should activate cooling centers and disseminate information through trusted channels.
<Callout type="warning">Do not ignore heat advisories; avoid strenuous outdoor activity during peak afternoon hours.</Callout>
