Spain’s military communications network is set for a major upgrade after Airbus Defence and Space and Thales Alenia Space won the contract to build SpainSat NG 3. The new geostationary satellite will replace the malfunctioning SpainSat NG 2, which suffered a space‑particle strike earlier this year. Scheduled for launch in the third quarter of 2030 and service in early 2031, the spacecraft will cover the Americas, Europe, Africa and the Middle East. Restoring this capability is critical for Spain and its NATO allies’ secure communications.

<Callout type="takeaway">SpainSat NG 3 will restore and upgrade Spain’s military communications after the loss of NG 2.</Callout>

## What happened
Airbus announced on July 31 that Hisdesat selected it as the prime contractor for SpainSat NG 3, tasking the company with building the satellite and providing the X‑band payload. Thales Alenia Space will supply the UHF and military Ka‑band payloads, delivering a multi‑band communications suite that expands bandwidth and improves anti‑jamming protection.

The satellite is slated for launch in Q3 2030 and entry into service in early 2031, operating alongside the existing SpainSat NG 1 to provide coverage from the Americas to the Middle East. The contract follows the loss of SpainSat NG 2, launched in October 2025 but rendered inoperable after a particle strike in January 2026. Hisdesat’s majority owner, Indra Group, indicated that insurance would cover the replacement cost.

In parallel, Airbus, Thales and Leonardo continue to pursue their Project Bromo joint‑venture, with antitrust filings pending in the European Union. The outcome of that venture could shape future European collaboration on defense‑grade satellites.

## Why it matters
Restoring Spain’s dedicated military communications restores a sovereign capability that NATO allies rely on for secure, jam‑resistant links. The enhanced payloads promise greater operational agility and resilience against electronic threats, addressing a vulnerability exposed by the NG 2 failure. Moreover, the contract reinforces Europe’s industrial base in high‑value defense space, showcasing a collaborative model that could influence future joint procurement.
