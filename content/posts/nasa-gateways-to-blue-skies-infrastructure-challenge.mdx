College teams across the United States are being challenged to redesign how we inspect the nation’s bridges, tunnels, and other critical structures. NASA’s 2027 Gateways to Blue Skies competition, announced today, asks students to propose airborne platforms that could make infrastructure inspections safer, faster, and cheaper. With the nation’s aging infrastructure threatening economic stability, fresh ideas from the next generation could reshape how engineers monitor and maintain vital assets.

<Callout type="takeaway">Airborne inspection concepts from students could dramatically improve the safety and cost of monitoring America’s infrastructure.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s University Innovation Project is sponsoring the Gateways to Blue Skies competition, selecting infrastructure inspection as the 2027 theme. The contest is open to multidisciplinary teams of two to six college students, who must submit a written proposal and a two‑minute video in Phase 1. Up to eight finalist teams will receive a $9,000 prize each and advance to Phase 2, where they will present updated work to NASA and industry experts at a forum in May 2027.

Winners of the Phase 2 presentations will be offered an internship with NASA Aeronautics during the following academic year, providing hands‑on experience in translating student concepts into real‑world aerospace applications.

## Why it matters
Infrastructure underpins the U.S. economy, yet many bridges, tunnels, and rail lines are over‑aged and costly to inspect using traditional ground‑based methods. Airborne platforms—drones, fixed‑wing aircraft, or hybrid systems—can reach hard‑to‑access areas without shutting down traffic, reducing downtime and safety risks for human inspectors. By tapping into fresh academic talent, NASA accelerates the pipeline of innovative technologies that could be adopted by the transportation and utilities sectors, potentially saving billions in maintenance costs.

## How to think about it
If you are a student or educator considering participation, start by mapping the specific inspection challenges of a target structure—such as bridge deck corrosion or tunnel lighting failures. Identify sensor suites (e.g., LiDAR, hyperspectral cameras) that can capture the needed data from altitude. Then evaluate flight‑time, payload, and regulatory constraints to shape a realistic aircraft concept. Finally, frame your proposal around measurable benefits: reduced inspection time, lower cost per mile, or enhanced safety metrics.

<Callout type="warning">Winning concepts are not guaranteed immediate deployment; they must still undergo rigorous testing, certification, and industry adoption processes.</Callout>
