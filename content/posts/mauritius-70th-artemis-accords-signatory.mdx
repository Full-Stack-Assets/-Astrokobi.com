In a quiet ceremony in the coastal town of Ébène, Mauritius added its name to a growing coalition shaping humanity’s return to the Moon. On July 17, Navindsing Jugmohunsing signed the Artemis Accords, making Mauritius the 70th nation to join the framework in just six years. The signing was attended by U.S. State Department officials and featured a video remark from NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson. For a small island developing state, the move signals a strategic push to leverage space for ocean protection, scientific collaboration, and economic opportunity.

<Callout type="takeaway">Mauritius becoming the 70th signatory shows the Artemis Accords are maturing into a truly global framework.</Callout>

## What happened
Mauritius signed the Artemis Accords on July 17 in Ébène, with the Ministry of Tertiary Education, Science and Research represented by Navindsing Jugmohunsing. U.S. State Department officials were present, and NASA Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson delivered remarks via video, emphasizing shared responsibility for peaceful and beneficial space exploration.

The signing follows Serbia’s accession a day earlier and marks the 11th new signatory this year. Since the Accords were launched in 2020 with seven original partners, the tally has risen to 70 nations, illustrating rapid international uptake.

## Why it matters
The Accords provide a common set of principles for deconflicting activities, using space resources responsibly, and sharing scientific data. Mauritius’ participation brings a small island perspective to discussions on protecting oceans and coastal environments from space‑derived threats. It also opens doors for Mauritian researchers and startups to engage in lunar and deep‑space projects, potentially diversifying the nation’s economy.
