NASA has opened media accreditation for two high‑profile launches slated for the coming months: the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on Aug 30 and the SpaceX Crew‑13 mission to the International Space Station in September. Both events promise groundbreaking science—the Roman telescope will deliver unprecedented panoramic views of the cosmos, while Crew‑13 will sustain the ISS’s research agenda. For journalists, gaining access means the chance to witness history and convey the excitement to the public.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s upcoming launches offer rare media access to cutting‑edge science and crewed spaceflight.</Callout>

## What happened
The Roman Space Telescope is scheduled to lift off no earlier than 7:20 a.m. EDT on Sunday, Aug 30, aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center. Named after NASA’s first chief astronomer, the observatory will capture deep, wide‑field images that could transform our understanding of dark energy, exoplanets, and the early universe.

Crew‑13 is slated for a mid‑September launch from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, using a SpaceX Falcon 9 and Dragon spacecraft. The crew—NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, CSA astronaut Joshua Kutryk, and Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov—will travel to the ISS for a six‑month science and research expedition.

Media accreditation deadlines are July 26 23:59 UTC for non‑U.S. journalists and July 30 23:59 UTC for U.S. journalists or U.S. citizens representing foreign outlets. Applications must be submitted through NASA’s online portal, and all requests are subject to review.

## Why it matters
The Roman Telescope will generate the deepest, widest images of the sky ever taken, enabling discoveries from distant supermassive black holes to faint dwarf galaxies. Public visibility of the launch can inspire a new generation of astronomers and bolster support for future missions.

Crew‑13 continues the commercial crew partnership with SpaceX, delivering fresh scientific payloads and maintaining the ISS’s role as a microgravity laboratory. Media coverage highlights international collaboration—NASA, CSA, and Roscosmos—all working toward shared scientific goals.

If you’re planning coverage, start by confirming your accreditation status well before the July deadlines.
