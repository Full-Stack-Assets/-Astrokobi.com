In the wake of a recent shooting, Maine's Democratic Senate hopefuls have united behind a call to abolish Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). The move comes as a surprise to many, given the complexities of the issue and the potential backlash from various stakeholders.

    <Callout type="takeaway">Maine Democratic Senate candidates are now unified in their support for abolishing ICE.</Callout>

    ## What happened
    The push to abolish ICE gained momentum following a shooting incident that drew national attention. While details of the incident are still emerging, it has sparked a renewed debate about the role of ICE and other law enforcement agencies in the United States.

    Graham Platner, a former Democratic nominee for the US Senate in Maine, had previously spoken out on issues related to immigration reform. Although his campaign was marred by controversy and he ultimately withdrew from the race, his stance on ICE reflects a broader shift within the Democratic Party.

    Shenna Bellows, the current Secretary of State of Maine and a candidate for the Senate seat, has also expressed her support for reforming or abolishing ICE. Her entry into the race has further galvanized support for the cause among Maine Democrats.

    ## Why it matters
    The move to abolish ICE has significant implications for immigration policy and law enforcement in the United States. Proponents argue that the agency has been responsible for numerous human rights abuses and that its actions have contributed to a climate of fear and mistrust within immigrant communities.

    However, critics argue that abolishing ICE would create a power vacuum that could lead to increased lawlessness and undermine national security. The debate is complex, with various stakeholders weighing in on the potential consequences of such a move.
