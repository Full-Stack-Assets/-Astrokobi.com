Today, ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot steps outside the International Space Station alongside NASA’s Anil Menon for a 6.5‑hour spacewalk. The EVA, scheduled to begin at 8:35 a.m. EDT, will replace a critical space‑to‑ground antenna. Adenot’s walk marks the first time a French woman has performed an extravehicular activity. The mission also represents the 282nd spacewalk in the ISS’s history, underscoring the continued need for on‑orbit maintenance. Watching the live broadcast offers a glimpse of international cooperation in action.

<Callout type="takeaway">Sophie Adenot becomes the first French woman to conduct a spacewalk, highlighting a new milestone for European human spaceflight.</Callout>

## What happened
Coverage of the EVA began at 7:00 a.m. EDT on Space.com, with the astronauts exiting the airlock at 8:35 a.m. EDT (12:35 GMT). Adenot, a crew member of SpaceX’s Crew‑12 mission that arrived in February, served as crewmember 2, while Menon, who launched on a Soyuz on 14 July, acted as crewmember 1.

The primary task is to replace a space‑to‑ground communications antenna that links the ISS to the Mission Control Center in Houston. NASA officials noted that the antenna is essential for high‑speed data transmission between the station and ground. The spacewalk will last about 6.5 hours, bringing the total number of ISS EVAs to 282.

Menon’s second EVA follows his first on 6 August with astronaut Jessica Meir. A brief sensor anomaly on his suit during that earlier walk was resolved, causing a short delay to today’s schedule.

## Why it matters
Adenot’s historic walk showcases progress in gender representation within European spaceflight, inspiring future generations of scientists and engineers. The joint ESA‑NASA effort reinforces international partnership essential for maintaining the aging ISS infrastructure. Replacing the antenna restores a vital communications pathway, ensuring reliable data flow for scientific experiments and crew safety.
