Space video games aim to let players explore the cosmos, but the sheer size of the universe makes realistic distances a design nightmare. The observable universe stretches about 93 billion light‑years, while our Milky Way spans roughly 100 000 light‑years. Even the fastest human‑made craft, Orion’s 25 000 mph trans‑lunar injection, would need about 80 000 years to reach Alpha Centauri. Developers must therefore compress astronomical scales into something a player can navigate without losing the sense of awe.

<Callout type="takeaway">Realistic cosmic distances are too vast for current games, so designers must deliberately shrink scale.</Callout>

## What happened

On a 1‑to‑10 billion scale, the Sun would be about the size of a large grapefruit, Earth a ball‑point tip about 15 meters away, and the Moon only 4 centimeters distant. Dr. Jeffrey Bennett notes that on this scale the outer planets could be “walked to in only 10 minutes,” yet reaching the nearest star would require a trek across the United States.

Simulating even a tiny fraction of the observable universe would push today’s hardware to its limits. Developers therefore rely on techniques such as procedural generation, level‑of‑detail scaling, and narrative shortcuts to give the impression of vastness while keeping performance viable.

## Why it matters

When games misrepresent cosmic distances, they can reinforce public misconceptions about space travel and the challenges of interstellar journeys. At the same time, thoughtful compression preserves the awe of exploration and keeps games accessible, striking a balance between education and entertainment.

## How to think about it

When designing a space game, start by defining the narrative distance you need and then choose a scale that fits gameplay loops—often a 1‑to‑10 billion ratio works well. Use visual cues, lore notes, or in‑game encyclopedias to remind players that the distances are dramatically compressed, preserving scientific context while maintaining fun.

<Callout type="warning">Do not present in‑game travel times as scientifically accurate without a clear disclaimer.</Callout>
