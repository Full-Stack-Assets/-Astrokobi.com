In June 2026, NASA’s Science Activation (SciAct) program gathered community college instructors for a virtual workshop aimed at modernizing the widely used OpenStax Astronomy 101 textbook. Over two weeks, five educators each enhanced an opening chapter with active‑learning modules drawn from NASA resources such as Treks, Universe of Learning, and Cosmic Data Stories. The pilot material will be field‑tested in fall‑semester classrooms, offering a proof‑of‑concept that a traditional textbook can become an interactive learning platform. By preserving the textbook’s clear narrative while adding immersive activities, the effort seeks to boost student engagement and comprehension of fundamental astronomical concepts.

<Callout type="takeaway">Integrating NASA’s active‑learning resources into OpenStax turns a static textbook into a dynamic classroom experience.</Callout>

## What happened

Three project teams from the SciAct program—NASA Community College Network (NCCN) led by the SETI Institute, Infiniscope led by Arizona State University, and NASA Treks—convened a virtual workshop in June 2026. The teams outlined a plan to embed NASA’s extensive visualization and activity libraries into the OpenStax Astronomy 101 framework while retaining its core explanatory text.

During the subsequent two‑week sprint, each of the five community college instructors selected one of the textbook’s opening five chapters and layered it with active‑learning modules. Resources such as NASA Treks virtual field trips, Universe of Learning simulations, and Cosmic Data Stories data visualizations were woven into problem sets, discussion prompts, and interactive labs.

The instructors presented their enhanced chapters in a wrap‑up session in July 2026. The modules are slated for field‑testing in community college classrooms during the Fall 2026 semester, providing early feedback on student outcomes and instructional feasibility.

## Why it matters

Adapting a free, widely adopted textbook with NASA’s cutting‑edge resources bridges the gap between static content and the interactive experiences students now expect. Successful pilots could set a precedent for other institutions to revitalize legacy texts, expanding access to high‑quality, NASA‑backed educational material without additional cost. Improved engagement and deeper conceptual understanding may translate into higher retention rates in introductory astronomy courses, strengthening the pipeline of future scientists and informed citizens.

## How to think about it

Educators interested in adopting the approach should start by reviewing the publicly available SciAct resources and selecting a single chapter that aligns with their course schedule. Pilot the active‑learning activities with a small student cohort, gather feedback on usability and learning gains, and iterate before scaling to additional chapters. Maintaining the textbook’s narrative flow while interspersing interactive elements helps preserve continuity for students accustomed to traditional reading.

<Callout type="warning">The active‑learning modules are still in a pilot phase; instructors should test them in a limited setting before full adoption.</Callout>
