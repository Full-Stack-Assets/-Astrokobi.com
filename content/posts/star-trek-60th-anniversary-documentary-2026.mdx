When a cultural touchstone reaches six decades, the celebration often extends beyond the screen. To mark Star Trek’s 60th anniversary, a new documentary titled *The Sixty‑Year Mission* will open in Regal Cinemas on Oct 9, 2026. Co‑executive‑produced by series legend William Shatner and directed by Mark A. Altman, the film stitches together archival clips and fresh interviews. The release follows a series of global events, from the Rose Parade appearance to anniversary screenings of classic Trek movies. For fans of space storytelling and the broader science community, the documentary promises a curated look at how a 1960s TV show reshaped public imagination of the cosmos.

<Callout type="takeaway">The documentary offers a deep dive into Star Trek’s cultural influence as it marks its 60‑year milestone.</Callout>

## What happened
The franchise’s 60th birthday arrives on Sept 8, 2026, sparking worldwide fan gatherings, a Rose Parade float, and special screenings of *Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home* and *Star Trek: First Contact*. In tandem, the documentary—titled *The Sixty‑Year Mission: How Star Trek Changed Television And The World*—was announced as a co‑executive production by William Shatner. Written and directed by Mark A. Altman, the film debuts exclusively in Regal Cinemas on Oct 9, 2026.

The feature weaves archival footage with new commentary from original cast members such as William Shatner, Walter Koenig, and George Takei, alongside insights from later contributors like Jonathan Frakes and Brent Spiner. Interviews also include Gene Roddenberry’s son and contemporary actors who discuss the series’ lasting impact.

## Why it matters
Star Trek has long served as a cultural bridge between speculative fiction and real‑world space exploration, inspiring generations of scientists, engineers, and astronauts. By chronicling the series’ evolution, the documentary underscores how a 1960s television program helped normalize concepts like warp drive, planetary protection, and multicultural crews, themes that echo in today’s space policy and outreach. For the science‑curious, the film provides a narrative lens that connects pop culture to the broader trajectory of human space ambition.
