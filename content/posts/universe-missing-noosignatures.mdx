The search for life beyond Earth has long been divided into two camps: the hunt for biosignatures and the search for technosignatures. However, this approach may be overlooking a crucial aspect of the evolutionary process. A new paper proposes a new term, noosemiotics, to describe the search for 'noosignatures,' or structured traces left by a mind.

## What happened
Astrobiology has traditionally focused on two areas: biosignatures, which are chemical traces that suggest biological activity, and technosignatures, which are the observable products of advanced technology. However, this approach leaves a gap in our understanding of the evolutionary process. A new paper, "Signs and Signatures of Intelligence," proposes a new term, noosemiotics, to describe the search for noosignatures.

Noosignatures are structured traces that a mind leaves on a medium. They can be physical, such as stone tools or architecture, or signal-based, such as complex animal communication. The key characteristic of noosignatures is that they must remain detectable as the product of intelligence, even if we can't decipher what they mean.

Examples of noosignatures can be found on Earth. The Indus Valley script, for instance, is a physical residue of cognitive activity that can be differentiated from simply biological processes.

## Why it matters
The search for noosignatures has significant implications for our understanding of intelligent life beyond Earth. By focusing on the structured traces left by a mind, we may be able to detect intelligent life at an earlier stage of development than previously possible.
