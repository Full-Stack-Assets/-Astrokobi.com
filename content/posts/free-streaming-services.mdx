A recent survey of over 1,000 cord cutters revealed their preferences for free ad-supported streaming services. The results showed a clear hierarchy among the top options, with The Roku Channel receiving the highest level of support. The survey highlights the importance of device compatibility, content selection, and user interface quality in this growing segment of the entertainment market. Free ad-supported streaming television services have expanded rapidly in recent years, delivering extensive libraries of movies and television series supported entirely by advertisements. The findings illustrate the concentrated appeal of a handful of established platforms among cord cutters seeking no-cost alternatives to traditional cable subscriptions and paid streaming tiers. 
   <Callout type="takeaway">The Roku Channel, Pluto TV, and Tubi are the top free ad-supported streaming services among cord cutters.</Callout>
   ## What happened
   The Roku Channel launched in September 2017 as an offering from Roku Incorporated, and it has since grown by securing licensing agreements with major studios. Pluto TV originated in 2013 and was acquired by Viacom in 2019, while Tubi entered the market in April 2014 and was acquired by Fox Corporation in 2020. These services have expanded rapidly in recent years, delivering extensive libraries of movies and television series supported entirely by advertisements.
   The survey revealed that The Roku Channel received the highest level of support at 31.1 percent of respondents, followed by Pluto TV with 24.2 percent, and Tubi with 23.8 percent. The findings illustrate the concentrated appeal of a handful of established platforms among cord cutters seeking no-cost alternatives to traditional cable subscriptions and paid streaming tiers.
   The services have emphasized integration with the broader ecosystem, making it convenient for users who already rely on the hardware for their streaming needs. Over time, they have added dedicated sections for kids and family programming while maintaining a focus on ad-supported access to both classic and newer titles.
   ## Why it matters
